AI Tools Across Health Professions Training: Exposure, Evidence & Governance Map

A cross-discipline AI exposure and governance map across the health sciences center.

AI governance at a health sciences center should not start with a vendor list. It should start with an exposure map: which students, faculty, and trainees encounter which AI tools, in which settings, under which privacy, documentation, clinical, and affiliate-site rules.

AI exposure is expanding faster than institutional guidance. This resource helps leaders identify where AI is already present and what governance, privacy, BAA, documentation, and supervision questions must be answered before broad use.

Tool examples illustrate AI exposure and governance needs. Inclusion does not imply endorsement, procurement recommendation, clinical approval, or permission for use with protected data.

Nursing students, PT students, respiratory therapy students, dental students, and public health students encounter AI tools at rotation sites under affiliate-site governance frameworks that may differ from each other and from home-program guidance.
Overview

How AI enters the health sciences center

Schools

Medicine, Nursing, Allied Health, Dentistry, Public Health, and Graduate Studies.

AI types encountered

Clinical AI, educational AI, research AI, administrative AI, and consumer/general-purpose AI.

Governance layer

Privacy and BAAs, documentation, affiliate policy, human review, approved-use inventory, and escalation paths.

Local

AI already deployed at affiliate sites

Governance question: Which AI tools do trainees encounter at each affiliate site, and whose policies govern their use, the affiliate's or the home program's?

LCMC Health: Nabla

Systemwide ambient AI implementation integrated with Epic. Attestation and documentation standards are governed by LCMC policy.

Source: PR Newswire, Dec 3 2025: LCMC Health selects Nabla

Ochsner Health: DeepScribe

Ambient AI documentation across 4,700+ clinicians. Trainees rotating through Ochsner sites may be inside a site-governed AI documentation environment.

Source: Ochsner Health news release, Jul 30 2024

Epic AI

AI charting, note drafting, order queueing, patient-message drafting, handoff summaries, billing support, and scheduling-related workflows embedded in the EHR.

Source: Epic.com: Art for Clinicians

OLOL and other affiliates

AI governance policies and tool inventories are independently maintained. Public confirmation should be obtained before describing site-specific tools.

Disciplines

Governance questions by discipline

Medicine
Which tools have executed BAAs across affiliate sites, and what is the approved-use standard for each rotation environment?
Nursing
Do home-program orientations address AI-generated risk scores, documentation, predictive alerts, virtual nursing, and ambient monitoring?
Allied Health
Do PT, OT, RT, SLP, audiology, and radiologic technology students receive site-specific AI orientation before clinical rotations?
Dentistry
Who reviews AI-flagged dental radiology findings, and what documentation standard applies to AI-assisted patient communication or treatment planning?
Public Health
What standards govern AI-assisted surveillance, population analytics, community messaging, and public health research workflows?
Graduate Studies and Research
What rules govern literature synthesis, AI-assisted writing, coding, data analysis, grant intelligence, research disclosure, and unpublished data?

Before use

Five governance questions leaders should require

  1. Is the tool institutionally approved?
  2. Does it handle PHI, student records, unpublished research, HR data, financial data, or other sensitive information?
  3. Is human review required before output is used?
  4. Which affiliate policy applies?
  5. Where are concerns, failures, or privacy issues reported?
Evidence

Evidence tiers

FDA-cleared

AI-enabled medical devices authorized for clinical use by the FDA.

Peer-reviewed

Tools or categories with published clinical or educational outcome data.

Professional guidance

Categories addressed by professional bodies such as ANA, APTA, ASHA, AMA, or AAMC.

Emerging

Tools in active deployment without full peer-reviewed validation or standardized institutional guidance.

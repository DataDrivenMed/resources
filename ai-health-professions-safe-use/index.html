AI Across Health Professions Training: Exposure, Evidence & Safe-Use Questions

A cross-school AI literacy and safe-use resource for faculty, trainees, staff, and program directors across academic health sciences.

AI governance at a health sciences center should not start with a vendor list. It should start with an exposure map: which learners and faculty encounter which tools, in which settings, under which privacy, documentation, clinical, research, and affiliate-site rules.

AI exposure is expanding across clinical affiliates, classrooms, research workflows, simulation, imaging, documentation, administrative work, and general-purpose AI use. This page helps users ask the right questions before using AI with patient, student, research, or institutional data.

Representative examples are included for awareness. Inclusion does not imply endorsement, procurement recommendation, clinical approval, or permission for use with protected data.

How to use this resource.
This is an educational awareness resource. It is not an approved-tool list, procurement recommendation, clinical-use policy, or compliance determination. Faculty, trainees, and staff should follow institutional policies, affiliate-site policies, HIPAA requirements, IRB requirements, and program-specific guidance before using AI tools with protected data.
AI exposure now spans every health profession. The practical question is no longer whether AI will enter training environments. It already has. The safer question is whether each school, program, course, lab, and affiliate rotation has enough clarity about approval, privacy, supervision, documentation, and human review.

Affiliate-site AI exposure

Students and trainees may encounter AI through clinical affiliates even when their home program has not independently selected the tool. That makes affiliate-site policy clarity essential.

LCMC Health: Nabla

LCMC Health announced a systemwide ambient AI implementation powered by Nabla and integrated with Epic. Trainees may encounter AI-generated documentation inside site-governed workflows.

Source: LCMC Health selects Nabla

Ochsner Health: DeepScribe

Ochsner announced DeepScribe deployment across 4,700+ clinicians. Trainees rotating there may encounter ambient documentation under Ochsner rules.

Source: Ochsner selects DeepScribe

Epic AI workflows

Epic describes AI-assisted charting, note drafting, patient-message drafting, handoff summaries, billing support, queueing, and scheduling-related workflows.

Source: Epic Art for Clinicians

OLOL and other affiliates

Tool inventories and rules may differ by site. Programs should confirm whether students can observe, use, document, attest, or be evaluated inside AI-supported workflows.

General

General-purpose AI tools

Many users will first encounter AI through general-purpose tools rather than approved clinical systems. Examples include ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Grok, Perplexity, NotebookLM, and embedded AI in productivity software.

Safe-use rule

Do not enter PHI, student records, HR data, financial data, unpublished research data, or affiliate-site clinical information into personal or consumer AI accounts.

Faculty supervision

Faculty should not require students or trainees to use AI tools unless approval status, data handling, accessibility, disclosure rules, and grading expectations are clear.

Research caution

Research teams should clarify IRB status, sponsor rules, journal disclosure requirements, intellectual property risks, and whether a tool retains or trains on submitted content.

Before use

Five safe-use questions

  1. Is this tool institutionally or affiliate-site approved for this use?
  2. Will any PHI, student data, unpublished research data, HR data, financial data, or confidential institutional data be entered?
  3. Is there a BAA, contract, IRB approval, or policy basis for this workflow?
  4. Who performs human review before the AI output affects documentation, teaching, assessment, patient care, research, or operations?
  5. Where should concerns, errors, privacy issues, or unsafe outputs be reported?

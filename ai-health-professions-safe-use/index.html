AI Literacy & Governance Awareness Resource

AI Across Health Professions Training: Exposure, Evidence & Safe-Use Questions

A representative map of where students, faculty, and trainees may encounter AI across affiliate clinical sites, classrooms, research workflows, and general-purpose tools.

AI governance at a health sciences center should not start with a vendor list. It should start with an exposure map: which students, faculty, and trainees encounter which AI tools, in which settings, under which privacy, documentation, clinical, and affiliate-site rules.

AI exposure is expanding faster than institutional guidance. This resource helps faculty, trainees, and staff recognize where AI is already present so they can ask the right governance, privacy, and supervision questions before using it.

Tool examples are included to illustrate AI exposure and governance needs. Inclusion does not imply endorsement, procurement recommendation, clinical approval, or permission for use with protected data.

How to use this resource
This page is an educational awareness resource. It is not an approved-tool list, procurement recommendation, clinical-use policy, or legal or compliance determination. Faculty, trainees, and staff should follow institutional policies, affiliate-site policies, HIPAA requirements, IRB requirements, and program-specific guidance before using AI tools with patient, student, research, or institutional data.
AI exposure now spans every health profession. Because AI tools are evolving quickly across clinical, educational, and research settings, programs across the health sciences center benefit from a shared cross-school reference that helps faculty, trainees, and staff understand where AI exposure is occurring and what questions to ask. Nursing students, PT students, respiratory therapy students, dental students, and public health students encounter AI tools at rotation sites daily, under affiliate-site governance frameworks that may differ from each other and from any home-program guidance.
For You

What this means for you

This page maps AI exposure. The questions below help you act on what you find.

  • F
    Faculty Do not recommend AI tools for clinical, educational, or research workflows unless privacy, data security, and institutional rules are clear. If you are unsure whether a tool is approved for use with student or patient data at a given site, ask before using it.
  • T
    Trainees (students, residents, fellows) Do not enter PHI, student data, unpublished research data, or affiliate-site clinical information into consumer AI tools. Free accounts for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, and similar tools have no HIPAA coverage. If a tool at your rotation site generates AI-assisted content, ask your supervisor which rules govern its use at that location.
  • S
    Staff Be careful with AI-generated emails, reports, spreadsheets, student records, admissions data, HR data, and financial data. Consumer AI tools are not approved for institutional or protected data. Use only tools reviewed and approved through your program or IT.
  • P
    Program directors Ask affiliate sites what AI tools students encounter during rotations and what site-specific rules apply to students from external programs. This question is not yet answered consistently at LCMC, Ochsner, or OLOL for non-medicine programs.
  • I
    IT and compliance Build a visible approved-use and BAA inventory accessible to all schools. Program directors across nursing, allied health, dentistry, public health, and graduate studies currently have no mechanism to confirm whether a given tool is covered at a given affiliate site.

Local

AI already deployed at affiliate sites

Governance question: Which AI tools do trainees encounter at each affiliate site, and whose policies govern their use, the affiliate's or the home program's?

LSUHSC-NO trainees rotate through LCMC Health, Ochsner Health, and OLOL. Each system has made independent AI procurement decisions. Trainees from all programs encounter these tools in documentation workflows and clinical environments, often without home-program guidance on which rules apply.

LCMC Health Nabla

LCMC Health selected Nabla for systemwide ambient AI implementation, integrated with Epic. LSUHSC trainees rotating through LCMC sites may encounter AI-generated documentation in clinical workflows. Attestation and documentation standards are governed by LCMC's policy, not the home program's.

Source: PR Newswire, Dec 3 2025: LCMC Health selects Nabla for systemwide ambient AI implementation

Ochsner Health DeepScribe

Ochsner Health selected DeepScribe for ambient AI documentation across 4,700+ clinicians. Trainees rotating through Ochsner sites are inside an ambient AI documentation environment governed by Ochsner's policy, which may differ from any guidance their home program has issued.

Source: Ochsner Health news release, Jul 30 2024: Ochsner selects DeepScribe for 4,700 clinicians

OLOL / Other Affiliates Monitoring

AI governance policies at OLOL and other affiliate sites are independently maintained. LCMC is informally reported to be developing its own clinical AI policy for clinicians. As affiliate policies evolve, the need for home-program coordination increases rather than decreases.

Informal faculty report. Policy status unconfirmed in writing. No public announcement available at time of publication.

Epic AI (all affiliate sites)

Epic AI functions, including AI charting, note drafting, order queueing, patient-message drafting, handoff summaries, billing support, and scheduling-related workflows, are embedded in the EHR at LCMC, Ochsner, and OLOL. Trainees may encounter AI-assisted content as part of routine EHR interactions across all three sites.

Source: Epic.com: Art for Clinicians product documentation

Governance implication

Across health AI policy and regulatory guidance, a substantial share of operational responsibility falls on providers and health systems, including tool approval, patient-data protection, clinician oversight, documentation review, and user training. Affiliate governance does not cover the exposure of trainees whose home program has no AI governance framework. This applies to medical, nursing, allied health, dental, and public health students equally.

Medicine

School of Medicine, MD/DO trainees

Governance question: Which tools have executed BAAs across affiliate sites, and what is the approved-use standard for each rotation environment?

Medicine has the most mature clinical AI tool landscape. These tools are assessed in detail elsewhere on this resource hub and are listed here for cross-school comparison.

Thalamus / Cortex
AI used in residency application screening. Affects how medical students are evaluated before they know AI is involved. Full assessment: datadrivenmed.github.io/Thalamus
Nursing

School of Nursing, BSN and graduate programs

Governance question: What AI tools do nursing students encounter at clinical sites, and do home-program orientations address AI-generated risk scores, documentation, and predictive alerts?

The 2025 ANA Code of Ethics (Provision 7.5) explicitly recognizes that machine learning, augmented intelligence, and AI can influence clinical decision-making, patient data, alerts, and downstream records, and that nurses must understand these tools.

Nursing education and exam preparationPeer-reviewed
GoodNurse Evidence
AI tutor built for nursing students on NCLEX content including Next Generation NCLEX formats. University of Rochester School of Nursing (2025): users averaged 95.1% vs 88.8% for non-users in an ECG course (p=.0048). Education tool, not a clinical decision-support tool.
Shadow Health (Elsevier)
AI-enhanced virtual patient simulation used in nursing education. Generates synthetic patient cases for clinical reasoning practice. Governance questions center on AI-generated assessment content and faculty oversight of simulation design.
Clinical AI at nursing rotation sitesActive deployment
Epic AI nursing workflows Local
Epic AI functions support nursing documentation, shift summaries, clinical alerts, and order management. Nursing students at LCMC and Ochsner encounter these outputs in clinical rotations. Home-program guidance on scope and limitations is not yet standardized.
care.ai, virtual nursing and ambient monitoring
AI-assisted virtual nursing platform using room sensors for patient safety surveillance, fall detection, and escalation workflows. Nursing students may encounter this category of ambient AI in clinical environments without formal orientation to how it works or who bears escalation responsibility.
Predictive tools, fall risk, sepsis alerts, deterioration scores
AI-generated risk scores deployed across affiliate hospitals. Students who act on these outputs need training on model limitations, alarm fatigue, and escalation responsibility. Underlying models vary by hospital and are rarely disclosed to trainees.
AI-assisted medication and infusion systems
AI-assisted medication dispensing and smart infusion pumps with predictive dosing are deployed in acute care settings. These are medical device categories distinct from EHR-based AI and carry their own regulatory and governance requirements.
Allied Health

Allied health programs, PT, OT, RT, SLP, audiology, radiologic technology

Governance question: Do allied health students receive site-specific AI orientation before clinical rotations, and does any home-program document address the tools they will encounter?

Allied health programs represent some of the widest AI exposure gaps in health professions governance. Trainees rotate through affiliate sites where AI tools are already operational, in ICUs, imaging suites, therapy environments, and documentation systems, with no cross-site coordination framework.

Physical therapy and occupational therapyProfessional guidance, APTA 2025
Ambient documentation scribes APTA
Abridge, Nabla, Suki, DeepScribe, and therapy-specific scribes generate AI-assisted SOAP notes and functional limitation documentation. APTA issued a practice advisory in September 2025 covering documentation responsibility and legal and regulatory considerations. Attestation obligations apply equally to PT and OT students during clinical placements.
WebPT / Net Health AI documentation
Leading PT and OT EHR platforms have integrated AI documentation assistance, auto-populating functional limitation and impairment fields. AI-generated functional limitation language carries payer-compliance risk if not reviewed before attestation.
Motion analysis and gait analytics
Markerless motion capture and pose estimation tools are entering clinical PT use. Validation varies by task, camera angle, body type, and clinical diagnosis. Students interpreting AI motion outputs need training on failure modes and override criteria.
Remote therapeutic monitoring (RTM), 2026 CMS CPT expansion
AI-enabled RTM platforms track home exercise adherence and symptom flagging. The 2026 CMS update expanded RTM CPT codes (98979, 98984, 98985). Students in RTM workflows need to understand the billing and documentation obligations these systems create.
OT documentation AI
AI tools generating ADL goals, functional status notes, and home safety documentation for occupational therapy. AI-generated OT content requires clinician review before attestation. Scope-of-practice and payer-compliance risks apply to student use.
Respiratory therapyFDA-cleared tools in use
Etiometry FDA-cleared
One of the stronger ICU clinical intelligence examples relevant to RT workflows. FDA-cleared platform surfacing respiratory drive, sedation level, and hemodynamic stability to support extubation readiness. Clark et al. (2025, Journal of Pediatric Critical Care): 30% reduction in postoperative ventilation time, 20% shorter ICU stays, extubation failure dropped from 13% to 0% after implementation.
Hamilton INTELLiVENT-ASV
Intelligent closed-loop ventilation mode embedded in Hamilton Medical ventilators. Automatically adjusts settings based on patient-ventilator interaction data. RT students may learn inside environments where AI-supported ventilation influences care decisions. Understanding override logic and escalation responsibility is essential.
Protocol adherence and weaning dashboards
RT-specific dashboards display AI-assisted readiness indicators for spontaneous breathing trials and ventilator bundle compliance. Students need to know whether AI outputs are advisory or operational, a distinction that varies by hospital and is rarely explained at orientation.
Predictive deterioration and sepsis tools
AI-driven respiratory failure prediction and ICU deterioration alerts are active in affiliate ICUs. RT students encountering these alerts carry escalation responsibility without always having governance clarity on model accuracy or failure modes.
Speech-language pathology and audiologyProfessional guidance, ASHA
Dragon Copilot / Heidi / Nabla ASHA
AI clinical documentation tools used by SLPs in hospital and clinic settings. ASHA has issued specific generative AI guidance for audiologists and SLPs covering evidence-based practice considerations and clinical responsibility. Trainees using AI scribes carry the same attestation obligations as other clinicians.
Automated speech analytics
AI tools for speech recognition, fluency analysis, and voice analytics are entering clinical SLP practice. Bias by accent, language, age, and disability is a primary governance concern. Models trained on limited data may produce clinically misleading outputs for underrepresented patient groups.
SpeechVive
AI device for Parkinson's patients that detects speech patterns in real time and triggers improved vocal output. Meaningful clinical use case with direct student exposure during neurological rehabilitation rotations.
AAC and assistive communication AI
AI-supported augmentative and alternative communication tools. Equity, disability access, and patient data privacy are the primary governance concerns.
Audiology: hearing-aid fitting algorithms and automated audiometry
AI-assisted hearing-aid fitting and automated auditory screening tools are entering audiology practice. Device responsibility and patient-specific adjustment carry distinct governance requirements from software-based AI tools.
Radiologic technologyMost mature allied-health AI, 1,000+ FDA-cleared algorithms
Viz.ai / RapidAI FDA-cleared
Automated stroke triage and care coordination. Radiology technologists may trigger AI-assisted workflows around flagged scans before radiologist review, raising questions about scope, escalation protocols, and documentation responsibility for students in these environments.
Aidoc / Annalise.ai FDA-cleared
Radiology worklist prioritization and critical finding alerts. Students in departments using AI triage should understand how the prioritization model works and who bears responsibility for delayed reviews.
Image acquisition and QA AI
AI for positioning support, protocol selection, artifact detection, and dose optimization is integrated into imaging equipment. Students must understand which outputs come from regulated devices versus uncleared software.
FDA AI-enabled medical device list FDA
Radiology and imaging represent the largest category on the FDA AI-enabled medical device list. Third-party trackers report more than 1,000 radiology-focused AI authorizations. Students should know which clinical AI outputs come from regulated devices versus uncleared tools.
Dentistry

School of Dentistry

Governance question: Are dental students receiving orientation to AI-assisted radiograph findings before interpreting them independently, and does the school have a policy on AI-generated treatment recommendations?

Dental AI is one of the fastest-growing areas of health AI adoption, with AI-assisted radiograph analysis, caries detection, and treatment planning tools already in commercial deployment.

Dental AI, clinical and educationalRapidly developing
Overjet FDA-cleared
AI-assisted dental radiograph analysis for caries detection, bone loss measurement, and annotation. FDA-cleared for clinical use. Students interpreting AI-annotated radiographs need to understand model limitations and override criteria before accepting AI-generated findings.
Pearl / VideaAI / Diagnocat
AI dental radiology platforms for caries detection, periodontal assessment, and tooth segmentation. Who reviews AI-flagged findings, how false positives are communicated to patients, and what documentation standards apply to AI-assisted diagnoses, these are open governance questions for dental education programs.
AI treatment planning, orthodontics and implants
AI tools for orthodontic planning, implant positioning, and aligner simulation. Student reliance on AI-generated treatment options raises questions about independent clinical reasoning and informed consent processes.
AI patient communication overlays
AI visual overlays on radiographs for patient education and case presentation. AI-generated visual displays for patient communication require consent, accuracy verification, and explainability standards.
AI coding and billing (dental)
AI-assisted CDT code selection and claims management are entering dental practice. Students in environments using AI billing tools need to understand the compliance and documentation obligations these systems create.
Public Health

School of Public Health

Governance question: When public health students use AI-generated risk signals or surveillance outputs, is there clear guidance on verification, escalation, and public communication standards?

Public health AI extends beyond clinical settings into population data, surveillance infrastructure, and community communication. The CDC reported 103 AI solutions or use cases as of December 31, 2025, describing AI as central to outbreak detection, tailored interventions, and public health data modernization.

Public health AI, surveillance, analytics, and communicationEmerging institutional relevance
Disease surveillance AI
AI-assisted outbreak detection, syndromic surveillance, and wastewater analytics tools. Students working with public health surveillance data encounter AI-generated risk signals that carry questions about false positives, public communication protocols, and decision authority.
Population health analytics
AI risk stratification, hotspotting, and care gap identification. Bias, equity, and explainability are primary governance concerns. Models trained on historically underserved populations may reproduce existing disparities.
Public health messaging AI
AI-assisted plain-language health communication and multilingual outreach tools. Misinformation risk, cultural appropriateness, and community trust are governance concerns when AI generates public-facing health content.
Research and grants AI, NIH RePORTER, Dimensions, Scopus AI
Literature synthesis, data modeling, and grant intelligence tools. Reproducibility, disclosure, and data governance apply to AI-assisted research in public health as in other disciplines.
Research

Graduate studies and research

Governance question: Are graduate students and researchers using AI with research data that may contain PHI, and does the institution have disclosure and data-governance standards for AI-assisted publications?

Graduate students and postdoctoral researchers across all schools are among the heaviest users of AI tools, for literature review, scientific writing, coding, data analysis, and grant development. This use is largely without institutional governance and creates distinct risks around research integrity, data security, and PHI exposure.

Research and graduate student AI exposureNeeds governance attention
Literature synthesis, Elicit, Scite, Consensus, Semantic Scholar, Perplexity
AI-assisted literature review tools used across health sciences research. Primary concern: hallucinated citations and unreliable evidence synthesis presented as authoritative. Institutional guidance on citation verification is not yet standard at most academic medical centers.
Scientific writing, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot
General-purpose AI used for manuscript drafting, abstract writing, grant narrative development, and editing. Authorship, disclosure, and journal policy vary widely and change rapidly. Institutional standards for AI disclosure in research publications are not yet uniform.
Coding and data analysis, GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, Cursor, ChatGPT
AI coding assistants used for statistical analysis, data pipeline development, and bioinformatics. Reproducibility, code review, and PHI exposure in research data are primary governance concerns. AI-generated code that processes research data requires human review before publication.
Biomedical research AI, AlphaFold, imaging analysis, omics tools
Specialized AI for protein structure prediction, histopathology image analysis, and multi-omics data integration used in LSUHSC-NO research labs. AI outputs in these areas require specialist interpretation and validation before publication.
Grant intelligence, NIH RePORTER, Dimensions, Scopus AI
AI-assisted grant landscape analysis and funding opportunity identification. Competitive intelligence and responsible use considerations apply. Institutional review of AI-generated funding intelligence is not standard practice.
General

General-purpose AI, what students and faculty actually use

Governance question: Do students and faculty know that consumer AI accounts carry no HIPAA coverage, and is there institutional guidance on the difference between enterprise and consumer AI tool access?

Across every school, the most commonly used AI tools are free, personal, and general-purpose. They carry no institutional BAA, no PHI protection, and no training-specific governance. Their ubiquity is the baseline governance problem.

Consumer AI tools in academic and clinical useNo BAA, no PHI protection
ChatGPT (OpenAI) Consumer
Most widely used general-purpose AI across all health professions programs. Free consumer accounts have no HIPAA coverage. Used for case write-ups, study aids, documentation drafting, literature summaries, and clinical reasoning support across all schools.
Claude (Anthropic) Consumer
General-purpose AI used for writing, analysis, coding, and research support. Consumer accounts have no institutional BAA. Used by graduate students and researchers for literature synthesis and manuscript drafting.
Microsoft Copilot / Gemini / Grok / Perplexity Consumer
Consumer AI tools with significant adoption across health professions students. Some Microsoft enterprise AI services may be covered under Microsoft's HIPAA BAA when deployed under an eligible institutional agreement and properly configured. Consumer Copilot and personal AI accounts should not be treated as approved for PHI under any circumstances.
NotebookLM (Google) Consumer
AI-powered document analysis and synthesis tool. No institutional BAA. Use with research data or clinical materials creates PHI exposure risk.

The HSC-level argument

AI exposure is distributed across every school and affiliate site in this health sciences center. A shared BAA inventory visible across all schools, an approved-use framework communicable to trainees at each affiliate site, and a coordination mechanism that keeps pace with affiliate AI policy development at LCMC, Ochsner, and OLOL, these are HSC-level needs, not problems any one school can solve in isolation.

Before You Use

Five questions to ask before using any AI tool

Regardless of school, program, or setting, these five questions apply every time a student, trainee, faculty member, or staff member considers using an AI tool with clinical, educational, research, or institutional data.

Before using any AI tool, ask:
  • 1
    Is this tool institutionally approved? Check whether the tool appears on an approved-use list from your program, school, or IT. If no list exists, that is itself a governance signal, ask your program director or compliance office before using it with any protected data.
  • 2
    Does it handle PHI or other sensitive institutional data? If the task involves patient information, student records, unpublished research data, financial data, or HR data, the tool must have an executed BAA or equivalent institutional data agreement. Free consumer accounts do not meet this standard.
  • 3
    Is human review required before the output is used? AI-generated clinical notes, diagnostic suggestions, billing codes, risk scores, and research outputs all require verification by a qualified person before acting on them. Identify who is responsible for that review in your setting.
  • 4
    Which affiliate policy applies? If you are on rotation at LCMC, Ochsner, OLOL, or another affiliate site, that site's AI policy governs your use, not your home program's policy. Ask at orientation what rules apply to external trainees at that location.
  • 5
    Where can you report concerns? If an AI tool produces a clinically concerning output, a data privacy concern, or an unexpected failure, know your escalation path before you encounter the problem. At present, most programs do not have a named AI concern-reporting contact. If yours does not, note it for your program director.
Evidence

Evidence tiers used on this page

Tools and categories on this page are not equally validated. Four tiers are used consistently throughout.

FDA-cleared

AI-enabled medical devices authorized for clinical use by the FDA. Examples: Viz.ai, RapidAI, Aidoc, Overjet, Etiometry. Highest regulatory standard. Governance still required for deployment and trainee use.

Peer-reviewed

Tools or categories with published clinical or educational outcome data. Examples: GoodNurse (University of Rochester, 2025), Etiometry (Clark et al., 2025). Evidence base supports institutional confidence but does not eliminate governance requirements.

Professional guidance

Tools or categories addressed in guidance from APTA, ASHA, ANA, AMA, AAMC, or similar bodies. Examples: ambient scribes in PT (APTA 2025), generative AI for SLP/audiology (ASHA), AI in nursing (ANA 2025). Society guidance signals professional consensus without full clinical validation.

Emerging

Tools confirmed in active deployment without full peer-reviewed validation. Examples: Nabla at LCMC, DeepScribe at Ochsner, general-purpose AI across all programs. Highest governance urgency because exposure is real and institutional standards are not yet in place.
